pH of strong acids and bases

Exercises

  1. What is the pH of a 0.01 mol/L aqueous solution of HCl at 25 °C? (HCl is a strong acid.) (basic)

    Answer

    pH = 2.00

    Worked solution
    1. A strong acid ionizes completely, so [H⁺] equals its concentration. [H⁺] = 0.01 mol/L
    2. Take minus the base-10 logarithm. pH = −log₁₀(0.01) = 2.00
    3. At 25 °C, pH + pOH = 14. pOH = 14 − 2.00 = 12.00
    4. Compare with 7 (neutral within ±0.005 at 25 °C): the solution is acidic. pH = 2.00 < 7

    If you got −2.00, you dropped the sign: pH = −log[H⁺]. The pH of an acidic solution is positive and below 7.

  10. What is the pH of a 1 × 10⁻⁸ mol/L aqueous solution of HCl at 25 °C? (HCl is a strong acid.) (advanced)

    Answer

    pH = 6.98

    Worked solution
    1. So dilute that the ions of water itself count: [H⁺] = (c + √(c² + 4Kw)) / 2, with Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 25 °C. [H⁺] = (1 × 10⁻⁸ + √((1 × 10⁻⁸)² + 4 × 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴)) / 2 = 1.051 × 10⁻⁷ mol/L
    2. Take minus the base-10 logarithm. pH = −log₁₀(1.051 × 10⁻⁷) = 6.98
    3. At 25 °C, pH + pOH = 14. pOH = 14 − 6.98 = 7.02
    4. Compare with 7 (neutral within ±0.005 at 25 °C): the solution is slightly acidic. pH = 6.98 < 7

    If you got −6.98, you dropped the sign: pH = −log[H⁺]. The pH of an acidic solution is positive and below 7.

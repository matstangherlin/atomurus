Review

What to remember

  • pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]: each pH unit is a factor of ten in the H⁺ concentration.
  • Strong acids and bases ionize completely; weak ones only in part, as measured by Ka or pKa.
  • Strength and concentration are different things.
  • Neutral means [H⁺] = [OH⁻]; that gives pH 7 only at 25 °C.
  • A buffer of a weak acid and its conjugate base keeps the pH near pKa + log₁₀([A⁻]/[HA]).

Check yourself

  1. What is the pH of 0.001 mol/L hydrochloric acid?

    Answer

    pH = 3.00

    1. A strong acid ionizes completely, so [H⁺] equals its concentration. [H⁺] = 0.001 mol/L
    2. Take minus the base-10 logarithm. pH = −log₁₀(0.001) = 3.00
    3. At 25 °C, pH + pOH = 14. pOH = 14 − 3.00 = 11.00
    4. Compare with 7 (neutral within ±0.005 at 25 °C): the solution is acidic. pH = 3.00 < 7

  2. What [acetate]/[acetic acid] ratio gives a buffer of pH 5.00 (pKa = 4.756)?

    Answer

    [A⁻]/[HA] = 1.754

    1. Henderson–Hasselbalch: pH = pKa + log₁₀([A⁻]/[HA]), so log₁₀([A⁻]/[HA]) = pH − pKa. log₁₀([A⁻]/[HA]) = 5.00 − 4.756 = 0.244
    2. Undo the logarithm to get the ratio. [A⁻]/[HA] = 10^0.244 = 1.754
    3. A buffer works well while the ratio stays between 0.1 and 10 (pH = pKa ± 1). 0.1 ≤ 1.754 ≤ 10 ✓

  3. One solution has pH 3 and another pH 5. How many times more H⁺ does the first have?

    Answer

    A hundred times: two units, each a factor of ten.

  4. Is 0.001 mol/L hydrochloric acid a strong or a weak acid?

    Answer

    Strong: it ionizes completely. It is dilute, which is a different thing.

  5. Hot pure water has a pH below 7. Is it acidic?

    Answer

    No. It is still neutral, because [H⁺] = [OH⁻]; Kw is larger at a higher temperature, so the neutral pH is below 7.

Reading the periodic table — answer key

For the teacher. Do not hand it out before the activity.

Draft — under teacher review. Not published yet.

duration
50 min
level
High school — first year

Lesson plan (50 min)

  1. 5 min — Introduce the table: rows are periods and columns are groups.
  2. 25 min — In pairs, students answer questions 1 to 5 with the table open.
  3. 10 min — Discuss question 6 (hydrogen) with the class.
  4. 10 min — Go through the answer key together and note any questions left open.

Materials

  • A computer, tablet or phone with the Atomurus periodic table (or a printed table)
  • This sheet and a pen

Answers

  1. In which group and which period is iron (Fe)? (1 pt)

    Answer: group 8, period 4

    The group is the column and the period is the row the element is in.

  2. How many elements does the table class as alkali metals? Which ones? (2 pts)

    Answer: 6: Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr

    Hydrogen is in group 1 but is not an alkali metal (see question 6).

  3. How many noble gases does the table show, and in which group are they? (2 pts)

    Answer: 7, in group 18: He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn, Og

  4. Which element has atomic number 17, and what is its category by the table's colours? (1 pt)

    Answer: Chlorine (Cl) — Diatomic Nonmetal

    The site's table splits non-metals into «diatomic» and «polyatomic»; «non-metal» is also right.

  5. In period 2, how many elements are metals (alkali, alkaline-earth, transition or post-transition)? Which ones? (2 pts)

    Answer: 2: Li, Be

    Boron, right after them, is a metalloid, not a metal.

  6. Why does hydrogen usually sit at the top of group 1, yet it is not an alkali metal? (2 pts)

    Answer: Because it has a single electron, like the group-1 alkali metals, so it usually sits at the top of that column. But hydrogen is a non-metal and behaves very differently from sodium or potassium.

    Source: Atomurus, «What is the Periodic Table?», FAQ

Common errors

  • Swapping group (column) and period (row).
  • Counting hydrogen among the alkali metals.
  • Counting boron as a metal in period 2 (it is a metalloid).

Notes for the teacher

The answers to questions 1 to 5 come from the site's own table data (elements-data.js): if the classification changes, the generated key changes with it.
